Fairbanks just voted on data centers. Except it didn't.
On October sixth, about one in five borough voters cast a ballot.
The closest borough Assembly seat went to Alexis Howell by two hundred seventeen votes, unofficially.
Runner up Miguel Ramirez took no position and said there need to be more studies.
So who answered the data center question?
In August, that Assembly asked the state Legislature in Juneau to pause new commercial data centers on public land until standards exist, KUAC reported.
A data center bill on power use and decommissioning passed the state Senate fourteen to five, and the House never took it up.
Meanwhile a University of Alaska Fairbanks guide takes up the same subject.
Now the case against.
A pause until standards exist is a no with good manners if nobody drafts them.
On data centers in general, winning incumbent Nick LaJiness said, "I don't think that data centers are good for any community."
But the pause ends when standards exist. That's a door with a condition, and a condition can be written.
That guide is called Computing in Place, and the Navy's research office funded it, per the deposit record.
Per UAF, it asks who benefits, and who pays.
The guide's lead, Leasi Vanessa Lee Raymond, says, "A small facility supporting a hospital or tribal government, for example, raises different questions from a hyperscale facility serving global customers."
So the missing option isn't yes or no. It's rules sized to what gets built.
Fairbanks asked for rules first. The Senate's data center bill stalled in the House.
So who writes them?
