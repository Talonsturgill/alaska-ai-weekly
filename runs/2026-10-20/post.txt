The state may hand about 19,950 acres near Houston to AIDEA with no payment for land value. AIDEA's letter says no tenant is committed.

The state Department of Natural Resources (DNR) has preliminarily proposed conveying the land to AIDEA, the state's industrial development authority. AIDEA would still cover survey and closing costs. No final decision has been made.

AIDEA's plan lists data centers as one possible use.

In a July 30th letter its director, Randy Ruaro, wrote that there is no committed data center tenant, industrial developer, site plan, power source, cooling system, water supply plan or construction schedule. Alaska Public Media reports he later called a data center on the site implausible.

None of the sources reviewed mention AI. The link is that data centers are the buildings AI runs in.

That makes the data center the wrong question. The open question is what the land is for.

Houston's city council and the Meadow Lakes Community Council voted to oppose the transfer, according to the Mat-Su Sentinel. On October 14th the Senate Resources Committee voted to subpoena DNR Commissioner John Crowther, with five senators voting yes. He is set to appear on October 26th, when senators can put the question to him.

The best case for the transfer is real. It says developers want site control before they sign, so ready land can be how a state competes. AIDEA says it has declared about 495 million dollars in dividends to the state, but that figure is statewide and the letter says no return to Houston is guaranteed.

Land should not move until a use is named and a hearing has tested it. A transfer with no committed use is the moment to ask what ready land is ready for.

What use would you want on the record before this land moves, and who should decide it?
