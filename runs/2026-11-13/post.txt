AIDEA's board voted a company it owns twenty million dollars. No offering is open yet.
The real fight isn't build versus pause. It's which comes first, the rules or the money.

On November 9th, Governor Dunleavy announced InvestNorth, a state sponsored platform to finance infrastructure, pitched at data centers. It was built with Plural, a San Francisco company.

It will use tokenization, a digital record of who owns a stake. Some deals may be reserved for Alaska residents.

Plural's chief executive calls the infrastructure behind AI "one of the largest capital needs in a generation." That is the pitch. Not one of the sources reviewed here names an AI project.

The record is thinner than the pitch. No offering is open. The structure, timing and terms haven't been decided.

The Daily News counts at least seven meetings between the governor and Plural's chief executive. A critic quoted by the paper is the founder of an advocacy group. The critic calls it using AIDEA to move hundreds of millions of public dollars to Lower 48 corporations.

AIDEA's board has at least one more meeting on December 4th. On December 7th, Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins becomes governor and says he would support a pause on data center construction until the state adopts comprehensive rules.

Dunleavy said, "If there is an opportunity for data centers here in Alaska, let's do it right." Doing it right starts with the order. The residents only option is still a may. It helps Alaskans only if it is written into the rules before the first offering opens.

Should AIDEA's board wait on the rules before December 4th, or does moving first serve Alaskans better?
