LinkedIn caption

119 of 150 UAA students cited for academic misconduct used AI, per a UAA report via the Anchorage Daily News.

The Daily News reports the University of Alaska has no single AI rule. The academic integrity section of UAA's student handbook bars "utilizing devices not authorized by the faculty member." It never mentions AI. Faculty set AI rules course by course.

Those 119 are 1.1 percent of the more than 10,500 students who attended UAA last semester. A citation isn't a count of use. It shows how often a rule was enforced, and the rule changes at every door.

The same report says AI cases have taken over the majority of cases the conduct office addresses. It also shows a 73 percent increase in AI use for plagiarism last year.

There's a fair case for the doors. In a writing course, AI can let a student skip the learning. In UAA's master's program in AI, data science and engineering, AI is the subject. No single rule fits both.

Stephanie Erickson, a special projects lead, presented draft AI language to regents in September. Per the Daily News, she said, "We need to be deliberate with how we want to engage with AI instead of letting it just happen to us."

My read is to keep the doors and add a floor. The regents' principles should set a default that applies when a syllabus says nothing about AI.

The Daily News reports regents will consider guiding principles in November. That's a place to start. Guiding principles aren't a rule.

Should the university set one default for authorized AI use, or leave it to each faculty member?
