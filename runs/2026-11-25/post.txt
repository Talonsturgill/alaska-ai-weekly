12 days to a new governor, and 6,800 no-cost acres near Houston are still in play.
Rules first would stall no project AIDEA has named there.

Governor-elect Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins takes office December 7th. He asked AIDEA to postpone further action until then. The board of that public state corporation met behind closed doors for five hours and took no action. Its pending agenda carries no data center item.

The data center pieces moved earlier. The board voted $20 million to its own Alaska Data Corp this year, per ADN. And on November 10th the state approved giving AIDEA about 6,800 acres near Houston at no cost. In July the authority said that land could host logistics, manufacturing and "scalable data centers," according to the Alaska Beacon.

Requests to reconsider are due November 30th and DNR's 30 days to answer run past the handoff. Per ADN the next governor has said he'd support a data center moratorium until the state adopts rules. His team hasn't said whether he'd reverse this transfer.

The authority's director says it isn't rapidly moving through projects without due diligence. The parcel was cut from nearly 20,000 acres and returns to the state if AIDEA fails to develop it.

Against that sit more than 2,300 comments, mostly opposed. The Mat-Su Borough also asked the state to reconsider.

In July the authority wrote that the site had no committed tenant, power source, cooling or schedule. In November a spokesperson said it still hasn't identified a specific project, per the Alaska Beacon. Alaska can write its rules before a tenant arrives and court AI infrastructure money from firmer ground.

Should Alaska settle the rules before it lands a tenant, or the other way around?
