The Fairbanks borough's data center pause would run 18 months. Its research is due in 10.
The 8 months between decide whether this is readiness or refusal.

Mayor Grier Hopkins proposed a 12-month pause on permits for large data centers. The borough Planning Commission recommended 18 instead. The News-Miner reports the recommendation was unanimous.

Per the News-Miner, any one of three things would trip it. The project draws at least 5 megawatts, covers 40,000 square feet or more, or makes at least 5 megawatts on site. The planning director says 5 megawatts can power at least 3,000 homes.

That precision is the strength. The News-Miner reports the general use zone covers 97 percent of the borough. In the planning director's words, "Anything goes in general use." A fixed size line is the first firm limit in a zone where anything goes.

The reach is less settled. The borough likely can't zone inside the Eielson Air Force Base fence.

Mayor Hopkins told Alaska's News Source he believes a purely government purpose data center would not be subject to the pause. Private use might be different. The borough is still researching that gray area.

The people asking for time have real reasons. One commissioner warned that most of these facilities come with diesel generators. Another commissioner said they're hearing anxiety from people over AI in general. A resident told the commission it's smart to take the time.

Neither outlet reports why the pause would outlast the research.

Nothing is law yet. The Assembly's final vote is likely January 14th, and the clock would start then.

An 18-month pause with research due at month 10 needs a written job for the last 8 months. Rules a builder can meet, and an answer at the Eielson fence, are that job.

If you sat on the Assembly, what would the pause have to produce before month 18 ends?
