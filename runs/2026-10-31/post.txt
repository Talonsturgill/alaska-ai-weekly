Four undersea cables, two from 1999, and a $75 million ceiling with no route yet.
The vote buys an option on a cable, and no partner or project is picked.

On October 21st the AIDEA board voted to form Northstar Fiber Development, wholly owned by AIDEA.

It can put up to $75 million into subsea and land fiber.

Alaska reaches the Lower 48 on four undersea cables and one land route, per AIDEA. Three of the cables land in Oregon and one in Washington.

Two entered service in 1999, and AIDEA puts a typical design life at about 25 years.

AIDEA's release calls Alaska "a digital cul-de-sac where traffic must travel through the Lower 48 to reach the rest of the world."

The resolution says no cable from Alaska connects directly to Asia and puts Asia third, under "explore and collaborate."

The $75 million is a ceiling, not a check. The resolution lists a link to the Pacific coast first.

AIDEA's release says any capacity it buys has to land in Alaska and not just pass by.

Before any binding commitment the resolution requires a whole-project finance plan and binding customer commitments.

The authority runs out on December 31st, 2028, unless the board extends it.

Any AI reading of this vote is context. Neither the press release nor the resolution says artificial intelligence, data center or compute.

The economic studies the resolution cites cover the Nordic and European context, and it admits they haven't quantified Alaska.

A ceiling, conditions and a landing rule are how careful public money should look. AIDEA itself says two systems have reached the end of a typical design life.

The real test is the first customer who signs.

Until somebody signs, Alaska's traffic leaves on the same four cables and one land route. What would a binding customer commitment have to look like for Alaska to win from this?
