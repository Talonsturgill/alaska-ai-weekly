# Angle room proposals, 2026-10-02

## A. THE BOTTLENECK (celebratory)
Thesis: The narrow point in Alaska's groundfish science is a person at a microscope counting rings, 30,000+ a year (c2), and near-infrared plus machine learning widens it 600 to 800 percent per NOAA (c3) while keeping human readers in the process, so far more ages can reach the stock assessments managers use to set catch limits (c10).
Falsifiable if: the microscope never held ages back, or NIR ages can't be used in assessment models at all.
Against: no claim shows an NIR age inside a live assessment yet (c8 calls it the next step). Survives as capacity, not a changed limit.
Second movement: the archive (c4, c1, c6) reframes the slow human counting as the reason the machine works, the training set. Fair-case scene: no catch limit has moved yet; the handoff to the assessment is ahead.

## B. THE ARCHIVE (celebratory)
Thesis: The AI that ages Alaska groundfish works because people spent decades collecting, storing and counting rings on fish ear stones, so the archive is the hard-to-copy asset and the model is what it pays out.
Falsifiable if: the models worked out of the box with no aged archive behind them, or the speedup came from the spectrometer alone.
Against: the archive sat for decades without producing this; the engineering (c3, c4) made it pay. Survives: spectrometers and methods can be bought, sixty years of counted stones can't.
Second movement: c6, 2 million pairs in a 1930s wooden hangar, moved 2012 on an NSF grant. "The intelligence was stored before it was ever computed." Fair-case scene: give the engineers their scene, and the assessment step is still ahead (c8).
Note: don't claim the exact labelling method; say models learned from the archive human readers built and aged.

## C. THE HONEST LIMIT (curious)
Thesis: NOAA built a real, fast machine reader on a 2.5 million otolith archive, and it sits one stated step short of the catch-limit math with human ring counters still in the loop, a limit on how far the story goes, not a knock on the work.
Falsifiable if: a NOAA source shows NIR ages already feed a live assessment as the data of record, or ring counting dropped.
Against: undersells a clear win. Survives by placing the tool accurately on NOAA's own map.
Fair-case scene: speed is not accuracy; the human at the microscope stays (c9, c3); managers, not the model, set limits (c10).
