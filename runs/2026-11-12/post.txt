Fifty years, about 715 acres, and a state notice that never says AI.

AI is the headline label. The decision is half a century of state land for a campus that makes its own power.

On November 10th, Alaska's Department of Natural Resources approved a 50-year lease for STAK Energy under case ADL 422741. The site sits about 26 miles south of Deadhorse.

The state's notice calls it a gas powered data center with high performance computing units. It never uses the word AI. The Northern Alaska Environmental Center, an advocacy group, lists artificial intelligence computing among the possible uses. That is a list of uses, not the state's description.

The Anchorage Daily News reports a 1 to 3 gigawatt system that would burn North Slope gas and would not tie into the Railbelt grid. STAK says it expects no impact on the public grid or ratepayers, which is a company claim.

Then the public spoke. The state received more than 1,200 comments, and the Daily News says most were opposed. The governor-elect has said he would support a moratorium on data center construction, a statement of support and not an action, and one the report does not tie to this lease.

Put the two clocks side by side. World Oil reported a 15-year gas sale agreement for the campus. The lease runs 50 years.

A promise isn't a term. Fifty years of state land should come with written terms on grid impact before anything is built. Requests for reconsideration are due November 30th.

If a project powers itself and stays off the grid, what should Alaska ask of it in return for 50 years of state land?
