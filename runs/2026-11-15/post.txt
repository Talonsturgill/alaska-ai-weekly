Alaska takes records requests out loud. A new draft would bar ones filed through AI tools.
Nothing reported says where a tool ends.

State rules today say an agency can't refuse a request just because it skipped the form.

On November 11th the Department of Administration posted proposed changes to those rules. The Juneau Empire reports the draft would bar requests submitted through third party services or automated tools, including AI. Agencies could also require their own forms or portals.

No reason for the AI line appears in anything reported.

Picture someone who asks an AI helper to trim a rambling question down to one agency. They fix its mistake and send it in themselves. Is that an automated tool? The draft as reported doesn't say.

The rest of the reported draft goes after volume and access.
Agencies could stop work after 250 documents or 15 staff hours.
Past five hours of work in a month, the requester would pay for all of it.
The appeal window would drop from 60 working days to 30.
Residents could get priority over nonresidents.

State law says every person has a right to inspect a public record.

Those proposed caps would limit the work whatever tool filed the request. So the AI line doesn't manage volume. Until someone defines a tool, it only adds a test nobody can check.

The fair case is real. Staff hours are finite, and the Empire reports the filing names efficiency as its aim. Public interest fee waivers stay, and a required portal could put a known person behind each request.

The tool line needs a definition and a reason before it becomes a rule.

Where would you draw the line between a person using a tool and a tool filing on its own?
