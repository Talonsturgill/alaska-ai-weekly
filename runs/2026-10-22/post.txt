The real asset in this story is 45,800 line kilometers of state airborne surveys. The AI prospector has named no Alaska property.

On October 20th, Portland-based Geomorphic AI said it has set up Golden Dyke Exploration in Alaska. It calls itself an AI driven prospect generator that screens geological data for overlooked mineral systems. It says what it reads in Alaska is public.

Ganes Creek alone is 25,731 line kilometers, flown with a magnetic sensor and gamma ray spectrometers. Aniak and the Little Taylor Mountains add 20,090 more. Add the two and the lines run more than once around the Earth, though they were flown as separate surveys and not one trip.

The state offers both datasets for download, and says the Ganes Creek data will improve understanding of the geology and mineral potential. Alaska should keep flying and publishing surveys like these, because a map offered for download hands the edge to whoever reads it best.

What Geomorphic adds to it is so far a claim. Its release says the deposits it mentions belong to other people and don't show what's on its own ground. A map of magnetism and natural radiation shows where rock differs. Only a drill shows what the rock holds.

By its own account Geomorphic doesn't drill. Partners fund and run the drilling while it keeps an interest. Nothing reported shows the company used these two surveys, and every description of its AI here is its own.

If the state puts its surveys up for download, who should get to act on them first, and who should carry the cost of the first hole?
