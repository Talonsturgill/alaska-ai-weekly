The one sector Alaska's jobs forecast calls most vulnerable to automation is projected to lose 489 jobs. The report won't call it AI.

That refusal is the most useful line in it. The Alaska Department of Labor's October forecast adds 8,577 jobs by 2034. Its sidebar on AI says it's too early to predict the magnitude.

On insurance it calls AI losses too uncertain to project this year.

The 489 is down 8.1 percent, and banking and related services make up 75 percent of it. The report ties the loss to online services and says it isn't clear how much AI will accelerate the trend.

It doesn't attribute the louder rows to AI either. Reporters and journalists are projected down 27.9 percent, and it ties that to services going remote and work going freelance.

The forecast is built from historical trends, population and known projects. A labeled blank beats a number nobody could defend, and the next forecast should replace it with a published range on AI, method shown, so a banker or an adjuster can plan around it.

If you work in banking or insurance in Alaska, what would you want that range to tell you?
