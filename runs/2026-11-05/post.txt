UAA's report says 119 of 150 cited for misconduct used AI. Professors say Claude drafted UA's plan.
The better question is who was consulted, whoever held the pen. KTOO reported the draft got too little faculty and student feedback. The regents are set to take it up at their November meeting.

In an Anchorage Daily News column, UAA professors Gregory Hartley and Joel Potter say Anthropic's Claude generated the first draft and that it wasn't disclosed until October 9th. That is their claim, and no university response to it was found.

The university's technology chief told ADN he drafted the AI language over the summer at the regents' request. Both accounts can be true at once.

The same UAA report, per ADN, says AI plagiarism rose 73% last year. Today the university has no single AI policy across its campuses, per ADN. If approved, the September draft would require all three campuses to have an AI plan for students, faculty and staff.

A university official says AI could recommend but a human decides, and that the school should be deliberate about how it engages with AI. Using a tool for a first draft isn't a flaw. What counts is who reads the draft and who gets to change it.

The professors want the plan dropped. They write that trusting AI to write the AI plan is the wrong approach.

If a human decides, as the university says, would you rather know who wrote the plan or who was consulted?
