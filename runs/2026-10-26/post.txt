The Alaska Senate voted 14 to 5 for a data center bill, and the House never took it up.

That bill would have regulated how data centers draw power from public utilities and required decommissioning plans. Its sponsor Senator Löki Tobin returns to the Legislature next year. The Alaska Beacon expects the next governor to face similar legislation.

The next Legislature decides what comes next, and the fight is over size, place, power and who gets paid.

At a Fairbanks-area forum, twelve statehouse candidates generally agreed that big data centers need rules and local input, per the Fairbanks Daily News-Miner. Then they split on how. Three backed a state moratorium until Alaska can study the issue.

Jeff Rentzel favored some sort of tax. Aimee Moore said data centers could pay every Alaskan a digital dividend.

In South Anchorage, Ky Holland wants modest, purpose-built data centers while John Boyle is more open to large ones, especially on the North Slope. Both see a way to attract energy investment.

The governor candidates answered in September. Dave Bronson and Treg Taylor back data centers. Bernadette Wilson wrote that she will never allow Alaska to be littered with hyperscale ones. Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins wanted a moratorium until the Legislature passes a framework.

Early voting began October 19th and Election Day is November 3rd.

What should the next framework settle first? The size, the place, the power or who gets paid?
