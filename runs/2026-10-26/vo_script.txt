This fall, Alaska's candidates are being asked about data centers, and Election Day is November third.
At a Fairbanks area forum, twelve legislative candidates generally agreed that big data centers need rules and local input, per the News-Miner.
One bill already tried to write those rules down. Hold that thought.
Then the candidates split on how, and three backed a state moratorium until Alaska can study the issue.
Republican Jeff Rentzel favored some sort of tax, and Republican Aimee Moore said data centers could pay every Alaskan a digital dividend.
In South Anchorage, independent Representative Ky Holland wants modest data centers, and Republican John Boyle is open to large ones, especially on the North Slope.
For governor, Republican Dave Bronson said in September, I'm a big believer in data centers, and Republican Treg Taylor said, we need to embrace it.
Republican governor candidate Bernadette Wilson wrote in September that she will never allow Alaska to be littered with hyperscale data centers.
Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, running for governor, said in September he wants a moratorium until the Legislature passes a framework.
In the Interior, legislative candidate Harmony Tomaszewski said the grid isn't ready, and Mike Prax and Barbara Haney said zoning is local.
Yet Holland and Boyle both see data centers as a way to attract energy investment.
