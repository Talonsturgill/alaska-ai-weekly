Burgum said people want to drop ten billion on a data center. Two days earlier Golden Valley Electric's reserve fell under 15 megawatts.

On Wednesday October 14th Interior Secretary Doug Burgum told a Fairbanks Chamber of Commerce event that people are knocking on his door to drop ten billion on a data center with no tax breaks. The reports name no developer, no site and no megawatts.

On Monday Golden Valley Electric sent a Level 2 alert (its first under a new alert system). Level 2 means the spare room on the grid (the reserve) is under 15 megawatts.

One generating unit went offline while two others were in scheduled maintenance. The reports tie the alert to that unit and not to a data center.

Dollars and megawatts are different units. A pitch that names no megawatts isn't yet a proposal, and a ten billion dollar figure can't be weighed against a 15 megawatt reserve until someone names the load.

Burgum pointed to Ellendale (North Dakota) with homes and electric bill rebates per KTUU. A customer that brings its own power could leave more reserve and not less, but the reports don't say whether this one would.

Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins (a Democratic candidate for Alaska governor) was in the audience. He said "I think we should pause." He also said development can proceed "in a thoughtful way."

Burgum said "you convert electricity into intelligence, you make money." That sentence makes the AI question a power question first.

What would a data center have to put in writing in megawatts before you'd say yes?
