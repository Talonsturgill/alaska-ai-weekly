Sitka's planning commission voted unanimously to recommend banning data centers in every zone, and nobody has proposed one.

The vote came on October 21st, per KCAW. It is a recommendation, and the Sitka Assembly has no date set to take it up. The real flaw is the draft's missing size line.

The city's own memo names the driver. It says AI has spurred growth in the sector and that a small power hungry data center here is "not entirely unlikely."

The draft's definition sets no size line. It covers a fridge-size data center at 150 kilowatts in Cordova, per Sitka's memo. It also covers a gigawatt campus.

Sitka's record peak demand is 26 megawatts. One gigawatt is about 38 times that by simple division.

City staff wrote on October 16th that the draft did not propose prohibiting data centers. The draft allowed them only with a conditional use permit.

The sustainability commission backed that route unanimously on October 5th. The planning commission recommended a ban instead.

The ban has a real case. Sitka's grid is an island powered entirely by water. In 2025 its hydro plants made 120 gigawatt hours and the town used 118. Hydro moves with rain.

In public testimony Lisa Sadlier-Hart told the commission "We need to say it loud, and we need to say it now."

Fairbanks has proposed a line instead. Its mayor filed a moratorium on large data center permits through the end of 2027, with 5 megawatts of peak demand among the thresholds. A University of Alaska Fairbanks guide says data centers are not all the same.

A ban is one clear rule. A size line is a number someone has to choose. Which should Sitka pick, and if a line, where?
