$220 million, and not one claims process for the drivers whose data GM allegedly sold.
Consent is where the settlement bites, so the fixes start at the door.

Per the settlement, GM will pay $220 million. 36 attorneys general signed on and Alaska is one of them.

Alaska's share is $2.5 million per the Alaska Department of Law as reported by Alaska's News Source. GM admitted no violation of law.

Everything about conduct is alleged. The complaint says Smart Driver tracked hard braking, hard acceleration and late-night driving plus location every three seconds.

It says GM sold that to Verisk and LexisNexis.

It says those firms built driver ratings for insurers and that some insurers used that information in part to set rates.

The alleged break sits at intake. The complaint says GM's apps told over 370,000 people they weren't enrolled while data was collected. It attributes those notices to a technical glitch.

Most of the settlement's fixes act on that door. GM needs a driver's clear yes before it collects or shares driving data.

It must collect only what is reasonably necessary. It must delete driving data kept from before January 16th, 2025, with exceptions.

Acting Attorney General Cori Mills said "This holds GM accountable and reinforces our commitment to safeguarding Alaskans' personal information."

The limit is that none of this pays an individual driver. The settlement sets up no claims process for drivers.

Neither the decree nor the public releases state how many Alaska drivers were in the data.

Drivers can ask GM for a copy of their data or ask it to delete it.

If your car was in that data, what would a clear yes from you have to show before GM collects anything, and would you ask for the copy first?
