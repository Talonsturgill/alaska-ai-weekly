119 of 150 cited UAA students used AI. Both sides say AI helped draft UA's own AI plan too.
One plain disclosure line on the plan would settle who knew, and the draft's own rule already supplies it.

AI Engaged is the plan the University of Alaska regents asked for in May. They met in Fairbanks November 4th to 6th.

It sets six principles, four investment priorities and five expectations for every campus. Until now, professors set their own AI rules class by class.

Two UAA professors, Gregory Hartley and Joel Potter, wrote in an op-ed that Anthropic's Claude produced the first draft, and that this wasn't disclosed until October 9th.

Their line is "allowing AI to speak first sets the harmful precedent of favoring a chatbot's authority over human wisdom."

Five UA faculty answered on November 5th that "in this case, AI use was not concealed." They say anyone who met with administrators and board members knew how the first draft was built. The rebuttal doesn't deny AI was used.

Knowing depended on who was in the room, and a public document shouldn't work that way. UA special projects lead Stephanie Erickson describes the draft's own rule as AI can recommend and a person decides. One line saying AI helped draft the plan and a person decided applies that rule to the plan itself.

At UAA, 119 of 150 students cited for misconduct used AI. That is about 1.1% of more than 10,500 students, and these are cited cases, not a rate of misuse.

The film behind this post was produced with Claude. No decision on the plan had been reported as of November 6th.

What one sentence should the plan carry about how AI helped draft it?
