## What is happening in Alaska this month

AI data centers have become the biggest land-and-power argument in Alaska, on two
separate pieces of ground at once, and it has reached the governor's race.

### Track 1 — FEDERAL LAND (fully verified, see claims.json c1 to c15)

The Department of the Air Force is offering about 4,700 acres of federal land at Joint
Base Elmendorf-Richardson, Eielson Air Force Base and Clear Space Force Station for
commercial AI data center development. Solicitation AFCEC-26-R-0006, a REQUEST FOR LEASE
PROPOSAL, not a construction contract. Proposals closed June 29th. As of September 15th no
projects had been awarded.

Salcha resident Sarah Hollister, walking a trail to a pond outside Eielson, told reporters
the roar of jet fighters always reminded her of the sacrifices we make for freedom, but
the hum of a data center shows her we have completely lost our way. In late August the
Fairbanks North Star Borough Assembly put a request to halt large commercial data centers
on its legislative priorities, and KUAC reports it was the only item that appears on both
the state and the federal list. The reporting says the Assembly may be powerless, because
it can't tell the Pentagon what to do on military land.

Senator Lisa Murkowski: not a proponent of data centers at JBER or the Interior
installations, because Alaska can't support them on an insecure energy base.

### Track 2 — STATE LAND (PENDING VERIFICATION, a second validator is checking it now)

Alaska's Department of Natural Resources has proposed conveying nearly 20,000 acres of
state land near Houston, in the Mat-Su, to AIDEA for an industrial district whose marketed
uses include data centers. More than 2,000 public comments. The Mat-Su Borough Assembly,
the Houston City Council, the Willow Area Community Organization and the Meadow Lakes
Community Council have all voted unanimously against. Five state senators, bipartisan,
questioned the legality in a September 9th letter. Houston takes up a two-year moratorium
on October 8th. AIDEA's director says there are no data center plans for the parcel.

**TREAT EVERY FIGURE IN TRACK 2 AS UNVERIFIED UNTIL THE VALIDATOR RETURNS.** It may be
corroboration and escalation. It may not be load-bearing.

### Track 3 — THE GRID UNDERNEATH (partly verified)

Fairbanks already has some of the nation's highest utility costs (VERIFIED, c15). ENSTAR
has said Southcentral could run 18 days short of gas this winter (PENDING). A data center
regulatory bill passed the Alaska Senate 14-5 last session and the House did not advance
it before adjournment (PENDING); it would have required operators to carry their own
infrastructure costs and file decommissioning plans.

## THE THING THE FACT-CHECK FOUND, AND IT MAY BE THE STORY

The validator checked five readable sources for a megawatt figure, a water-consumption
figure, or any project size for the Alaska parcels. There are none. Its words: "residents
are objecting to water draw and round-the-clock noise from a project whose size has never
been published", and "THIS IS A FINDING AND IT IS ARGUABLY THE STORY".

The one water number circulating publicly comes from teenagers. Alaska Youth for
Environmental Action has been citing roughly 300,000 gallons a day for a medium data
center, which is a GENERIC INDUSTRY FIGURE and not a figure for any Alaska project. That
is not a criticism of them. It is evidence of the vacuum: the public is arguing with a
generic number because no specific one exists. **That figure is NOT in the safe set and may
not go on screen.**
