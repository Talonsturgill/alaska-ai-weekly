A Google AI answer told a Kodiak hunter snipe season had already started. It hadn't, and it cost her $150.

The Alaska Beacon reported it this week. She asked Google when snipe season opens, and according to the trooper's report the answer said September 1st. She shot three snipe on September 5th. The report says the general season didn't open until October 8th. At home she read the state regulations, saw her mistake and called troopers herself.

Then the trooper ran her exact search and got the same wrong answer.

I think she's the most useful person in this story. Her self-report is why we can see a wrong AI answer repeat instead of guessing that it does. A state spokesman told the Beacon he thinks it's the first time AI has been cited in a case like this. He expects more.

Weren't there warnings? There were. Google's own help page says AI answers may include mistakes and to check more than one place. Google didn't respond to the Beacon. The state's line is just as clear. The one source of truth is Fish and Game's regulations and emergency orders.

Here is the part I keep coming back to. Fish and Game's own online page says its regulations are simplified and that emergency orders can override them. So even the official page is a summary. What counts sits underneath. Read the regulations and any emergency order before you go out. Fish and Game will mail you the regulations, and its office takes calls.

I'm not against AI answers. I want Alaskans to win with them. A good answer to a hunting question would name its source and point to the emergency orders. Until then, treat the answer on top as a lead and not as the rule.
