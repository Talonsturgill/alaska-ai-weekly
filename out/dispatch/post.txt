A Google AI answer gave a Kodiak hunter the wrong snipe season, according to a trooper's affidavit. It cost her $150.

She turned herself in. Then the trooper ran her exact search and got the same wrong answer.

A search box shouldn't outrank the state's own regulation as the first answer a hunter sees.

She searched "snipe season" on her phone, and Google told her it began September 1st. She shot three snipe on September 5th. That is per the affidavit, as the Alaska Beacon reported it.

The affidavit says snipe weren't legal to take under the general season until October 8th. At home she dug into the state regulations, learned the season hadn't opened and called troopers.

Austin McDaniel, a Department of Public Safety spokesman, told the Beacon it's the first time he thinks they've seen AI cited. He expects more of it.

Google's own help page says AI responses may include mistakes and tells users to check important info in more than one place. Google didn't respond to the Beacon.

McDaniel put it plainly. The one source of truth for a hunt is Fish and Game's regulations and emergency orders.

Yet Fish and Game's own online page says its regulations are simplified and that emergency orders can take precedence. Printed copies are available by phone, email or the nearest office.

I want AI answers that work for Alaskans out on the land. Until they show the regulation and emergency order they came from, treat the answer on top as a lead and not as the rule.
