A finished report isn't a finished professional decision.

A gathered report and an editable draft deserve credit as real preparation. Judge a finished workflow by the professional decision that follows, not by the arrival of a page.

Koniag Government Services says it supports Wingman, a platform for custom digital assistants. Its September 11th announcement names contracting, logistics and financial management. KGS is a subsidiary of Koniag, the regional Alaska Native corporation associated with the Kodiak region.

Amazon Web Services (AWS) describes earlier Army procurement work in a case study. DORA, the Determination of Responsibility Assistant, starts with a contracting professional emailing a vendor identifier in the subject line.

It retrieves compliance records through APIs, including data from the System for Award Management. Prebuilt logic helps assemble a standardized report. The professional reviews that report and makes the determination.

Separate Jumpstarter tools use large language models through Amazon Bedrock. User inputs become initial sections of contracting documents. People refine and approve those sections.

These are the workflows described by AWS. They don't establish an independently measured productivity result for Wingman. The September announcement also doesn't quantify an Alaska return.

What would you check in an assembled report, or change in a generated draft, before putting it to work?
