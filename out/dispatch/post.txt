A diploma should still mean somebody earned it. That remains true even when AI helped along the way.

The University of Alaska Board is discussing AI Engaged today. It is still a draft, and that distinction matters.

The proposal says UA credentials should continue to certify competence a student earned. It also leaves curriculum judgment with UAA plus UAF and UAS. Faculty could set AI rules ranging from required to excluded based on course objectives.

That is the right starting point. AI can widen access and support practice. It can give faculty better tools. The credential still has to represent the work and judgment of the person whose name is on it.

The same standard gets sharper when AI influences consequential decisions. For predictive systems affecting admissions or financial aid as well as advising and HR analytics the draft calls for documented evaluation and meaningful human oversight.

Those words sound strong. They are not measurements. A serious implementation needs to define who can stop a system. It needs an evidence standard and a way for a student to challenge a result.

If adopted, institutions would get one year to build policies and plans. The clock has not started. A separate budget request should not be mistaken for funding this framework.

What evidence would make you trust that AI helped a student learn without weakening what the diploma certifies?
