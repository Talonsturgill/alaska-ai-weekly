A drone flew into an Alaska cloud. A model came back with about 19 million gallons.

That happened on Alaska's Kenai Peninsula on August 23rd. Rainmaker says two Elijah drones made ten sorties across seven coordinated missions and released nineteen silver iodide flares. The operation is impressive. The headline needs a closer look.

The radar saw seven features that the company manually tracked. From there the analysis assumed a five dBZ background subtraction and ran twenty seven radar to precipitation relationships. The company mean was 57.62 acre feet. That is about 18.8 million gallons and it became the rounded nineteen million gallon claim.

That number is not a bucket count. It is a model result built from real radar observations plus company interpretation. The reported fifth to ninety fifth percentile range runs from 41.70 to 89.35 acre feet. Even that range does not capture every source of uncertainty.

The lowest radar beam sat roughly 1,800 to 2,200 meters above mean sea level. The company report did not describe ground gauge or tracer confirmation at the surface. Rainmaker argues that a saturated column made loss below the beam less likely. Independent research from the SNOWIE project shows cloud seeding can produce snowfall under suitable conditions. That is a meaningful physical precedent. It is not a Kenai ground truth check.

My read is simple. The drones demonstrated a real Alaska robotics capability. The radar recorded a real signal. The water volume remains a company estimate. The next campaign should finish the measurement all the way to the ground.

What evidence would you need before calling an estimated weather gain a measured one?
