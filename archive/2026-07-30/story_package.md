# STORY PACKAGE — Dispatch 2026-07-30

## The story

On 2026-07-22, NOAA Fisheries published a PROPOSED incidental harassment authorization for the
Office of Naval Research's Arctic Research Activities in the Beaufort and eastern Chukchi Seas,
Year 9. Citation: 91 FR 46055-46079, document 2026-14816.

The activity runs September 2026 through September 2027 and supports the Arctic Mobile Observing
System (AMOS), the Office of Naval Research's effort to make the ice-covered Arctic observable and
navigable by autonomous machines year-round.

Comments close 2026-08-21. The docket is still open as of this run.

No Alaska outlet has reported this. The research fan-out found no 2026 coverage of AMOS anywhere,
and NOAA's own incidental-take index page did not yet list it.

## The hardware, exactly as the notice states it (agency maximums, not estimates)

- Up to 6 fixed moorings carrying WHOI Micro-modem acoustic sources at 900 Hz
- 2 drifting Ice Gateway Buoys
- Up to 1 REMUS 600 uncrewed underwater vehicle (Micro-modem at 900 to 950 Hz)
- Up to 20 Argo-type autonomous profiling floats in the central Beaufort Sea
- Autonomous sea gliders, maximum dive depth 1,000 m
- Ice-tethered profilers, autonomous weather stations, ice mass balance buoys

## How the machines know where they are (this is the heart of the story)

GPS does not reach under sea ice. The gliders are buoyancy-driven and navigate by acoustic
trilateration off the moored 900 Hz sound sources, falling back to dead reckoning when the
navigation signal is unavailable.

VERBATIM from the notice, safe to quote:
"Autonomous sea gliders are intended for use in extended missions in ice-covered waters. Gliders
are buoyancy-driven, equipped with satellite modems providing two-way communication, and can
transit to depths of up to 1,000 m."

VERBATIM from the notice, safe to quote:
"Hibernating gliders would continue to track their position, waking to reposition should they
drift too far."

## The ships

- R/V Sikuliaq, operated by the University of Alaska Fairbanks, deploys the gear in September 2026.
- VERBATIM from the notice: "The R/V Sikuliaq is not an icebreaking ship but an ice strengthened
  ship, so it would not be icebreaking."
- CGC HEALY may do the recovery, and "icebreaking may be required." HEALY's maximum speed while
  breaking 1.07 m of sea ice is 5.6 km/hr.

## The animals

NOAA proposes to authorize Level B harassment ONLY, of beluga whales (Beaufort Sea and eastern
Chukchi stocks) and ringed seals (Arctic stock). No serious injury or mortality is anticipated.

HARD LIMIT: the authorized take COUNTS were not extracted. Do NOT state a take number anywhere.
Say "Level B harassment only" and nothing more precise.

## The honest AI angle

There is NO machine learning in this notice. None. This is autonomy and acoustic engineering.
The film must say so plainly on screen, the way the 2026-07-26 dispatch said no AI was involved
in the voter-roll story.

Robotics is explicitly inside this channel's beat, so this is not a stretch. The genuinely
interesting thing is the engineering: the Navy is building a synthetic sound-based positioning
system for a place where satellites cannot see, because ice is in the way.

## Alaska stakes

- The work happens in the U.S. Exclusive Economic Zone directly off Alaska's North Slope.
- These are waters Inupiat communities hunt. Beluga and ringed seal are subsistence species.
- The deployment ship belongs to the University of Alaska Fairbanks.
- The comment window is open until 2026-08-21 and no Alaska outlet has told anyone.
