The DeepGreen Cook Inlet idea is clever. It is also still paper.

The company proposes 330 to 350 marine turbines and 66 subsea compute hives near Nikiski. The turbines would primarily serve the underwater data center. Spare electricity could reach the Railbelt through a proposed shore connection.

That physical fit deserves a serious look. Put the primary load beside its proposed power source. Use passive seawater cooling. Carry power and fiber ashore through one cable. The federal notice places the estimate at 100 megawatts.

Now comes the part that matters for Alaska.

The filing names no customer or committed financing. It does not prove output from the turbines. It does not settle the grid path or the economics. It does not establish the environmental effects.

FERC has opened the application to public comment. It has not approved construction. A preliminary permit would provide study priority and nothing more.

Comments and competing applications are due November 2nd at 1 PM Alaska time. Brief comments can be filed without prior registration.

The best response is neither hype nor dismissal. Test the engineering. Test the grid connection. Test the economics. Test the environmental case. Then ask whether the value really stays here.

What proof would you require before calling this an Alaska win?
